Fortune is a Treasure card from Empires. It doubles your current amount of  when you play it, which is a potentially extremely powerful effect — so much so that it only works once per turn, and even then its cost of $8,8D makes it arguably the most expensive card in the game. It is part of a split pile, with 5 copies of Fortune sitting under 5 copies of Gladiator, and it rewards you with free Gold if you gain it with a Gladiator in play.

## card text

+1 Buy

Double your $ if you haven't yet this turn.

When you gain this, gain a Gold per Gladiator you have in play.

## Official FAQ

- You only double your $ the first time you play a Fortune in a turn; any further times only get you +1 Buy.

## Other rules clarifications

- Fortune does not take into consideration \$ that has been produced, but already spent this turn, such as via Storyteller; Fortune only doubles the $ that you currently have when you play it.
- Coffers can be used before playing Fortune, and Debt can be repaid afterwards.
- If you put your +\$1 token (from Training) on the Gladiator/Fortune pile, you'll get +\$1 from playing Fortune before you double your $.
- If you buy Ball, gain a Cavalry, and draw a Fortune, you can play Fortune while you have your –\$1 token. The token takes effect after doubling your \$. So if you have \$5 and then play Fortune, you go to \$10, lose \$1, and end with $9.
- If you play Fortune when you have \$0, you won't remove your –\$1 token, and it will still count as doubling your $ for the turn.
- Unlike Fool's Gold, which checks if you played another Fool's Gold this turn, Fortune only checks if you haven't doubled your \$ this turn. So if your 1st Fortune gets blocked by a Highwayman, playing a 2nd Fortune will still double your $.
- When you gain Fortune, it cares about how may Gladiators you have in play, not how many you have played this turn. For example, if you play a Gladiator twice with Throne Room, it is still only one copy in play.
